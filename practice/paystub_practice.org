This practice trains the same skills as the Pay Stub Calculator, on a
smaller problem: variables and assignment, arithmetic, ~type()~,
~round()~, ~str()~, ~len()~, string repetition with ~*~, comparison operators,
and ~print~. No ~if~, no loops, no function definitions.

A program is a chain of statements: each step uses the results of the
one before. So do the parts *in order*, run the cell after each part,
and compare your output with the expected output *before* you go on.

Open a new Jupyter notebook and title it =Receipt_Practice.ipynb=. The
first cell is a Markdown cell with a title, your name and an AI-use
statement.

The program is driven by two values:

#+begin_example
  price    = 4.50    # dollars per coffee
  quantity = 6       # cups
#+end_example

** Part 1 - Subtotal

- Assign =price= and =quantity= as above.
- Compute =subtotal = price * quantity=.
- Print =subtotal= and its ~type()~ on two labelled lines:

#+begin_example
Subtotal = 27.0
Type = <class 'float'>.
#+end_example

** Part 2 - Tax and total

- Assign =tax_rate = 0.07= (7% as a decimal - *not* =7=).
- Compute =tax_amount = subtotal * tax_rate= and
  =total = subtotal + tax_amount=.
- Wrap =tax_amount= and =total= in ~round()~ with =2= decimals: money
  is rounded to the cent. Leave the tax rate alone - it is not money.

** Part 3 - A readable receipt

Define =title = "Coffee Shop Receipt"= and make a separator of dashes
*exactly as long as the title* with ="-" * len(title)=. Build each line
with ~+~ and ~str()~ - the numbers must come from your variables, not
be typed inside the quotes. Expected output:

#+begin_example
-------------------
Coffee Shop Receipt
-------------------
Subtotal: $27.0
Tax (7%): $1.89
Total:    $28.89
-------------------
#+end_example

Then print the results of two checks (a comparison gives ~True~ or
~False~):

#+begin_example
Is total greater than zero? True
Is total greater than subtotal? True
#+end_example

** Part 4 - Bulk price

The first 10 cups cost the full price. Every cup *beyond* 10 costs only
0.8 of the price. Extend your program for an order of *14* cups:

- the first 10 cups are paid at full price;
- the extra cups are paid at 0.8 times the price;
- the subtotal is the sum of the two;
- tax and total follow exactly as before;
- the receipt gains a =Full price:= line and a =Bulk price:= line above
  =Subtotal:=.

The 10 and the 0.8 need their own named variables (=bulk_limit=,
=bulk_rate=). Assume the customer always orders at least 10 cups - you
have not covered ~if~ yet. Expected output:

#+begin_example
-------------------
Coffee Shop Receipt
-------------------
Full price: $45.0
Bulk price: $14.4
Subtotal:   $59.4
Tax (7%):   $4.16
Total:      $63.56
-------------------
#+end_example

*Write Part 4 as a single code cell,* with a comment header at the top:
the script's purpose, your name as =Author=, and the =Input= and
=Output= variables.
