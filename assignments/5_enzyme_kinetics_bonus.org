* Enzyme Kinetics Calculator

An enzyme speeds up a reaction by converting a substrate into
product. How fast it does that - the *reaction velocity* - depends on
how much substrate is available, and on two constants that describe
the enzyme itself: =Vmax=, the fastest the enzyme can possibly go (once
it's completely saturated with substrate), and =Km=, a constant with
units of concentration that describes how much substrate it takes to
get the enzyme going at a reasonable clip.

You're building a small toolkit of functions that reports reaction
velocity for a given enzyme and substrate concentration. As with the
savings calculator, this one doesn't hand you the arithmetic - look up
the *Michaelis-Menten equation* if you don't already know it. It's one
line, and it's exactly the kind of formula a working scientist looks
up rather than memorizes.

Open a new Jupyter notebook and title it =Enzyme_Kinetics.ipynb=.

** What to build

Three functions, each with a docstring naming its parameters and
stating what it computes:

- A function that computes and prints the reaction velocity for a
  given =Vmax=, =Km=, and substrate concentration, using the
  Michaelis-Menten equation.
- A function that takes just =Vmax= and =Km=, and calls the function
  above at the *one specific substrate concentration* where a
  well-known fact about this equation kicks in: when substrate
  concentration equals =Km=, the velocity is always exactly half of
  =Vmax=, no matter what =Vmax= and =Km= actually are. (You should be
  able to see why, once you have the formula in front of you.)
- A function that takes =Vmax=, =Km=, and a substrate concentration
  (give this last parameter a sensible default), and calls both
  functions above to print a full report: the enzyme's constants
  first, then the velocity at the given substrate concentration, then
  the half-saturation check as confirmation.

Print velocities rounded to two decimal places; include units
(=mM= for concentration) in your output.

For =Vmax = 100.0=, =Km = 5.0= mM, and a substrate concentration of
=10.0= mM, calling your report function should produce exactly:

#+begin_example
Enzyme kinetics: Vmax = 100.0, Km = 5.0 mM
Reaction velocity: 66.67 at [S] = 10.0 mM
Check: at [S] = Km, velocity should be half of Vmax (50.00):
Reaction velocity: 50.00 at [S] = 5.0 mM
#+end_example

Then call your report function again on the same enzyme, *without*
specifying a substrate concentration, and confirm your default kicks
in cleanly (any reasonable default value is fine, as long as the
velocity line and the half-saturation check both still print
correctly).
