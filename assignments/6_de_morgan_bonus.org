* De Morgan's Laws

*Bonus exercise - worth 5 points.* It is available until midterm.

In class you combined booleans with ~and~, ~or~ and ~not~. Two rules
tell you how ~not~ moves through a combination. They are named after
the logician Augustus De Morgan:

1. ~not (A and B)~ is the same as ~(not A) or (not B)~
2. ~not (A or B)~ is the same as ~(not A) and (not B)~

In words: /"not both" means "at least one is missing"/, and /"not
even one" means "both are missing"/. In this exercise you *prove* the
two laws - with the computer as your helper - and then use one of them.

** Task 3: Proof by checking

- *Create* a level-2 headline that says =Task 3: Proof by checking=.
- Two booleans have only 2 x 2 = 4 combinations. If two expressions
  agree in all four, they are the same - that is a proof. But you
  should not have to compare the columns with your eyes.
- *Write* a check for the first law. Complete this skeleton: =ok= starts
  as ~True~ and is set to ~False~ if the two sides *differ* in any
  combination (~!=~ means "is not equal"). Never set it back to ~True~
  inside the loops.

  #+begin_src python :eval no
    ok = True
    for a in (True, False):
        for b in (True, False):
            if ...:            # the two sides differ
                ok = False
    print("First law holds:", ok)
  #+end_src

- *Do* the same for the second law.
- Expected output:

#+begin_example
First law holds: True
Second law holds: True
#+end_example
